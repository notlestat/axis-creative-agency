# Concept 05

Score 1-10: insight strength, originality, brand relevance, product integration, emotional impact, stopping power, simplicity, memorability, performance potential, production feasibility. These are judgments, not test results. Every score needs a reason. No automatic winner.

## Name

Tomorrow sends it back

## One-line idea

A task passed to an imagined tomorrow comes straight back with a request for one small start today.

## Consumer insight

H09: putting a task in tomorrow can feel like giving it to a different, more capable person. UNVALIDATED, related to H01 but a distinct explanation.

## Big idea

Make postponement a physical handoff that fails because the future owner is still you.

## Mechanic

Personification and temporal collision. An off-screen tomorrow returns an over-elaborate plan. The person replaces it with one concrete first action on the pad. The future is an implied partner, not a science-fiction setting.

## Hook

A hand slides a task marked for tomorrow out of view; it immediately slides back.

## Story

The owner tries to send the whole problem away. It returns, unchanged. They stop passing it around, write a manageable starting action on One Line and do that part. The rest can still wait. A series can explore different harmless tasks without claiming urgency.

## Brand role

A humane reminder that a small beginning can be more useful than an imaginary perfect future self.

## Product role

The pad changes the size and specificity of the handoff. It must show a next action, not merely a date.

## Why now

A recurrent behaviour hypothesis, not evidence of a trend. The idea would need audience validation and care around legitimate reasons to defer work.

## Why this brand

A one-task sheet can physically carry the smallest useful instruction. The concept could be copied by other list products; product identity would need strengthening.

## Emotional response

A knowing smile about the imagined future self, with permission to do a little rather than everything.

## Visual possibility

An ordinary edge-of-frame handoff could imply tomorrow without showing time travel. Art-direction and continuity choices remain open.

## Platform fit

Compact visual loop may work in short social. Time-related labels risk reliance on text, and the handoff could look like a second person rejecting work.

## Why it could work

The repeated return creates attention, while the reduced action gives the product a role in resolution. It offers a different emotional territory from mocking elaborate rituals.

## Risks

Could shame people with real constraints or imply that deferring is always wrong. Hands and object continuity are generation risks. The temporal metaphor may not read immediately.

## Originality notes

Temporal self-conversations and return-to-sender devices are familiar. This combination has not been cleared against current advertising. Avoid Get a Mac-style named character debates.

## Performance notes

Test whether the viewer understands tomorrow without spoken explanation and whether the product appears as a practical choice. Do not add guilt or fake deadline copy to force conversion.

## Pre-mortem and refinement

Failure: the return reads as a boss rejecting work, not an imagined tomorrow. Probability high, impact high. Mitigation: test the premise in a simple concept card before visual development. Refinement: one clear temporal cue and no new character. Reject if the story needs multiple explanatory titles.
