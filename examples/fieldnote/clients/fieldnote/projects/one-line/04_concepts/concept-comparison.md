## Concept summaries, strengths and weaknesses

| Concept | Idea | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| [01 The committee of one](concept-01.md) | An everyday task waits while its owner chairs an increasingly elaborate meeting about starting it. | The problem, comic escalation and product function are connected. The opening gives a specific reason to watch rather than a generic productivity promise. | Character continuity and multiple roles increase Melius difficulty. |
| [02 Too good to use](concept-02.md) | A perfectly organised work setup is admired as an exhibit until one useful, messy line spoils the display. | It confronts the product-category risk directly: stationery can be collected instead of used. It creates a product-centred action without promised performance gains. | May mock stationery enthusiasts or make the pad seem disposable and unnecessary. |
| [03 The endless receipt](concept-03.md) | A to-do list spills out like an absurdly long receipt while one actual next action fits on the pad. | Product function and visual mechanism are unusually easy to connect. It gives performance copy a clear proposition without stealing the Big Idea. | Could be mistaken for a receipt/budget app. |
| [04 No opening ceremony](concept-04.md) | Someone prepares a grand opening ceremony for an ordinary task, then begins before the ceremony can happen. | It makes the proposed insight into a simple behaviour rather than a claim. The product can participate early, and the ending resists the usual flawless productivity transformation. | A literal ribbon-cutting payoff would celebrate the ritual and reverse the message. |
| [05 Tomorrow sends it back](concept-05.md) | A task passed to an imagined tomorrow comes straight back with a request for one small start today. | The repeated return creates attention, while the reduced action gives the product a role in resolution. It offers a different emotional territory from mocking elaborate rituals. | Could shame people with real constraints or imply that deferring is always wrong. |

## Recommendation and tradeoffs

I recommend developing 04, No opening ceremony, if you prefer a distinctive visual idea with a simple product connection. The uncut ribbon gives the ending a turn: the person starts without celebrating the start.

03, The endless receipt, is the clearer product demonstration and the practical alternative if immediate comprehension matters most. Its mechanism is more familiar. 02 offers the gentlest emotional territory but may need a stronger opening. 01 and 05 carry greater casting/continuity or explanation risk.

## Kill-your-darlings critique

The favourite can fail. If we make the ceremony entertaining and give the pad only a closing pack shot, we have a ribbon joke rather than an ad for One Line. If the ribbon gets cut, the idea argues for the ritual it means to question. A future concept check must test the unused ribbon and product role before art direction.

Discarded approaches: a paper-product technology launch was too close to the inspected Bookbook mechanic; a perfect-desk transformation repeated the assumed category promise; a magical vanishing list suggested an unsupported productivity outcome. These were evaluated and removed before the five final territories.
