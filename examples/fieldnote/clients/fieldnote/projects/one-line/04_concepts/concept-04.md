# Concept 04

Score 1-10: insight strength, originality, brand relevance, product integration, emotional impact, stopping power, simplicity, memorability, performance potential, production feasibility. These are judgments, not test results. Every score needs a reason. No automatic winner.

## Name

No opening ceremony

## One-line idea

Someone prepares a grand opening ceremony for an ordinary task, then begins before the ceremony can happen.

## Consumer insight

H01: the rituals around starting can become more attractive than the unglamorous first action. UNVALIDATED.

## Big idea

Treat starting as an event that does not need an event.

## Mechanic

Provocation and inversion: imagine every small task required a ceremonial launch. The humour comes from abandoning the spectacle, not completing it. One Line names the modest action that can happen immediately.

## Hook

A ceremonial ribbon waits across an ordinary desk. The task behind it is comically small.

## Story

A person has arranged a disproportionate opening ritual. Before it begins, they write one practical next action on the pad and get on with it. The unused ribbon remains as a quiet joke. Further executions can abandon other unnecessary starting ceremonies without adding bigger spectacles.

## Brand role

An ally that lets the person in on the joke. The owner chooses to start; the brand does not lecture them.

## Product role

The pad replaces the ceremonial agenda with a specific action. It needs to be involved in the decision, not appear after an unrelated ribbon gag.

## Why now

The moment before starting is perennial. No claim about a current productivity trend or event.

## Why this brand

One line can act as the smallest possible opening statement. This links the campaign platform to the product format, though no proprietary advantage is established.

## Emotional response

Warm absurdity and a small sense of release. The action feels ordinary enough to copy.

## Visual possibility

The collision of modest work and unnecessary ceremony offers a strong single image. Exact props, casting and visual treatment require approval.

## Platform fit

A visible ceremonial object may create immediate curiosity in short social. A static version can retain the uncut ribbon and the actual started task. No forced 15-second beat sheet yet.

## Why it could work

It makes the proposed insight into a simple behaviour rather than a claim. The product can participate early, and the ending resists the usual flawless productivity transformation.

## Risks

A literal ribbon-cutting payoff would celebrate the ritual and reverse the message. Ceremony props can overwhelm the pad. The visual gag could be read as a new-office opening.

## Originality notes

Ceremonial exaggeration is a known comic technique. The distinct move is not cutting the ribbon and beginning anyway. No exhaustive market originality search has been performed; the concept is promising, not cleared.

## Performance notes

Test first-frame interpretation, pad recognition and whether the audience understands why the ribbon is left unused. Copy should clarify the modest start without explaining away the humour.

## Pre-mortem and refinement

Failure: production makes the ceremony more enjoyable than starting, and viewers miss the inversion. Probability medium, impact high. Mitigation: the ribbon is never cut and the practical action receives the payoff. Refinement: keep the ceremony small enough that the pad can matter. Kill if the idea only works with a long explanatory VO.
