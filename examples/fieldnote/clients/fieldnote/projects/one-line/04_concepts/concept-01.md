# Concept 01

Score 1-10: insight strength, originality, brand relevance, product integration, emotional impact, stopping power, simplicity, memorability, performance potential, production feasibility. These are judgments, not test results. Every score needs a reason. No automatic winner.

## Name

The committee of one

## One-line idea

An everyday task waits while its owner chairs an increasingly elaborate meeting about starting it.

## Consumer insight

H01: giving preparation an official-looking process can make avoidance feel responsible. UNVALIDATED.

## Big idea

Make private over-planning visible as a miniature bureaucracy, then reduce it to one written next action.

## Mechanic

Bisociation: personal planning meets a committee meeting. A single person assigns themselves chair, secretary and subcommittee roles. The pad becomes the one-item agenda that finally ends the meeting.

## Hook

The owner calls a meeting to discuss sending one ordinary email. The disproportion is readable without a productivity claim.

## Story

The preparations grow more formal while the original task stays small. The owner notices the absurdity, writes the first concrete action on One Line and begins. The ending is modest, not a miraculous finished day. A campaign can repeat the mechanism with different harmless everyday tasks.

## Brand role

A warm observer of work rituals, offering a small practical intervention rather than a scolding authority.

## Product role

The pad changes the scene by replacing the expanding agenda with one action. It must appear before resolution, not only as a pack shot.

## Why now

An ordinary recurring starting moment, not a claimed cultural trend. Current relevance still needs audience evidence.

## Why this brand

A single-task product can credibly interrupt a meeting that keeps adding agenda items. Another stationery product could imitate the gag, so distinctive product design would matter.

## Emotional response

Self-recognition with affectionate embarrassment, followed by relief at a smaller next step.

## Visual possibility

Possible domestic bureaucracy built from familiar work objects. Exact casting, lighting and camera decisions await approval.

## Platform fit

A visual excess-versus-one-action gag may work in short social film. Role changes could confuse a small-screen viewer; static extension can contrast an absurd agenda with one written action.

## Why it could work

The problem, comic escalation and product function are connected. The opening gives a specific reason to watch rather than a generic productivity promise.

## Risks

Character continuity and multiple roles increase Melius difficulty. It may feel like office humour for a narrow segment. Compressing the meeting can consume time needed for product comprehension.

## Originality notes

Personification and comic dialogue are familiar mechanics. The Get a Mac card was a warning against two-character product comparisons. This is one person staging a bureaucracy, but no exhaustive originality search was performed.

## Performance notes

Test whether viewers recognise the original task in the first two seconds and can name the pad at the end. Keep only one escalation. No CTR or sales forecast.

## Pre-mortem and refinement

Failure: people remember the committee but not the pad. Probability medium, impact high. Mitigation: make the expanding agenda and the One Line action the centre of the idea. Refinement: remove departmental characters and keep one owner. Kill if a simple concept test still reads as a meeting-software ad.
