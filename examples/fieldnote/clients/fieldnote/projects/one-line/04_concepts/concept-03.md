# Concept 03

Score 1-10: insight strength, originality, brand relevance, product integration, emotional impact, stopping power, simplicity, memorability, performance potential, production feasibility. These are judgments, not test results. Every score needs a reason. No automatic winner.

## Name

The endless receipt

## One-line idea

A to-do list spills out like an absurdly long receipt while one actual next action fits on the pad.

## Consumer insight

H01/H02: documenting everything can feel satisfying while making the first action harder to find. UNVALIDATED.

## Big idea

Give the cost of endless preparation a physical form, then make the next action visibly small.

## Mechanic

SIT multiplication and scale contrast. A list becomes an unwieldy continuous paper receipt. One Line holds a single concrete action beside it; the person chooses that action rather than reorganising the roll.

## Hook

A paper list keeps arriving long after the person has enough to begin. The physical excess creates the joke.

## Story

The list accumulates, inviting one more category and subcategory. The person stops adding to it, names a first action on the pad and starts. The long list remains, making clear that the product has not magically removed their responsibilities.

## Brand role

A practical voice that recognises overwhelm without pretending a small pad solves it.

## Product role

Its single-task format provides the visual counterweight. The action must be concrete and visible; a blank pad would reduce it to a size comparison.

## Why now

An everyday planning moment with no reliance on news or trends. Test the actual relevance with the audience.

## Why this brand

The format can credibly isolate one next action. Differentiation from scrap paper remains weak unless the actual product has a recognisable design.

## Emotional response

A slightly ridiculous sense of excess, followed by a manageable choice.

## Visual possibility

The relation between a long paper roll and a small usable pad is the territory. No approved palette, lens or shot sequence yet.

## Platform fit

Readable as a short film or static image. The first frame can carry the contrast. Small written details should not be required to understand the idea.

## Why it could work

Product function and visual mechanism are unusually easy to connect. It gives performance copy a clear proposition without stealing the Big Idea.

## Risks

Could be mistaken for a receipt/budget app. May imply fewer obligations or productivity improvement. A continuously growing paper prop may bend or duplicate in Melius.

## Originality notes

Think Small was used to inspect scale-as-argument; do not copy its layout or language. Multiplication and oversized lists are common. Originality score is capped at 6 pending a current creative audit.

## Performance notes

Test product-category recognition and whether viewers describe the promise as choosing a next action rather than finishing everything. Keep obligations visible after the choice.

## Pre-mortem and refinement

Failure: viewers think this is about spending money. Probability medium, impact high. Mitigation: establish a recognisable task-list context rather than retail checkout. Refinement: keep the long list present in the resolution. Reject if the receipt association consistently overwhelms the task idea.
