# Concept 02

Score 1-10: insight strength, originality, brand relevance, product integration, emotional impact, stopping power, simplicity, memorability, performance potential, production feasibility. These are judgments, not test results. Every score needs a reason. No automatic winner.

## Name

Too good to use

## One-line idea

A perfectly organised work setup is admired as an exhibit until one useful, messy line spoils the display.

## Consumer insight

H06: preserving the appearance of being organised may feel safer than making something imperfect. UNVALIDATED.

## Big idea

Treat untouched preparation as a museum object, then make the first mark the act that brings it back into use.

## Mechanic

Bisociation: the polished desk becomes a collection of unused objects. The twist is a person using the pad instead of admiring it. The brand values use over immaculate presentation.

## Hook

A tiny exhibit label calls the immaculate setup "Ready to begin". Nothing has actually begun.

## Story

The setup looks complete enough to display, yet the task remains untouched. A hand writes an ordinary, imperfect next action on One Line and leaves the space looking used. The work starts outside the display. Extensions can celebrate a crumpled used sheet rather than a pristine unused one.

## Brand role

A stationery brand willing to show its product in use and slightly spoiled. It must avoid preaching against people who enjoy attractive objects.

## Product role

The single line is the visible break in perfection. Its usefulness, not a premium material claim, gives it meaning.

## Why now

A familiar tension between owning work tools and using them. No trend or audience prevalence is asserted.

## Why this brand

A disposable task sheet can invite an unprecious first mark. Actual tear-off quality and product construction remain unverified mock details.

## Emotional response

Tender recognition and permission to be imperfect, with a quiet visual joke.

## Visual possibility

An exhibit-like arrangement could give way to an ordinary hand-marked page. These are territory possibilities, not approved art direction.

## Platform fit

Strong static potential and a simple film reveal. The museum reading may require too much text for the first second; art direction must make the setting legible.

## Why it could work

It confronts the product-category risk directly: stationery can be collected instead of used. It creates a product-centred action without promised performance gains.

## Risks

May mock stationery enthusiasts or make the pad seem disposable and unnecessary. The exhibit device can look like generic luxury-product advertising. The handwritten mark is a generation risk.

## Originality notes

The Lamp card was used to examine emotion around objects; this idea avoids its discarded-object pathos and tonal insult. Museum framing is a known device, so novelty remains limited.

## Performance notes

Check if viewers understand the shift from admiration to use without reading a long label. A product detail and action should remain identifiable in the static extension.

## Pre-mortem and refinement

Failure: the audience feels judged for liking stationery. Probability medium, impact high. Mitigation: make the owner fond of the objects and show use as pleasure, not correction. Refinement: the first mark is useful rather than vandalism. Kill if audience feedback reads as contempt for the category.
