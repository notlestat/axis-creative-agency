# Creative research

Read intake and the client's existing knowledge. Run `prepare <client> <campaign> research`. Use available read-only search tools, official brand/product pages, provided documents, reviews and accessible ad libraries. Search current competitor creative when requested. Record access failures; absence of access does not mean absence of activity.

Complete product, audience, category, competitor and creative reference research, then the research summary and evidence log. A source record needs URL or local document, date, what was actually inspected and the precise supported statement. Separate brand claims from independent proof. Actual quotations need exact wording and an accessible source. No synthetic customer quotes.

For competitors, capture advertiser, ad/library ID if available, date seen, platform, format, hook, offer, product role, visual properties, CTA, similarities and differentiation opportunity. Do not infer spend, targeting, effectiveness or profitability from ad longevity. For creative references, record what to study and what not to copy; describe composition/light/pacing rather than proposing a replica.

FACT = supported assertion. OBSERVATION = inspected detail. INFERENCE = interpretation linked to evidence. HYPOTHESIS = testable explanation with a validation plan. UNKNOWN = not established. Sampling and coverage limits remain visible in the research summary.

If personas help organise evidence, read `skills/axis-audience/SKILL.md`; it does not collect research or establish real customer traits. Do not invent demographic detail to fill a template.

For the mock test, use only declared fictional product assumptions and clearly labelled hypotheses. Mark research NOT_CONDUCTED. It may proceed to mock strategy and concepts for workflow testing; that exception never establishes a real product claim.
